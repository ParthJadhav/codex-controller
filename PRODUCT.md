# Product

## Platform

macOS desktop

## Users

Codex Controller is for macOS users who spend substantial time in Codex and want a DualSense to become a dependable physical command surface. They use it while reviewing code, navigating tasks, starting Codex's dictation and voice chat, and dispatching repetitive actions without breaking focus.

## Product Purpose

The product turns a DualSense connected over USB or Bluetooth into a visible, configurable control layer for Codex. Success means users can understand the controller’s current state at a glance, select and edit a physical control directly on the 3D model, move and click the macOS pointer with the touchpad, hold one button to run Codex's own push-to-talk dictation and reach voice chat from a held layer, and trust that mappings, safety rules, light status, and permissions behave consistently.

Dictation and voice belong to Codex. Codex Controller posts the commands and captures no audio of its own for them; it owns the controller, the mappings, and the honesty of the feedback.

## Positioning

The controller is the interface: physical input, mapping state, active layer, and feedback stay synchronized on one spatial object instead of being explained by detached diagrams.

## Brand Personality

Precise, tactile, and calm. The app should feel like a focused macOS instrument with the material confidence of high-end controller hardware, not a gaming dashboard or a novelty companion app.

## Anti-references

Avoid flat controller illustrations, floating hotspot chips, leader lines, neon gaming HUD conventions, ornamental glass panels, oversized rounded cards, and interfaces that hide controller or permission failures behind generic status copy.

## Design Principles

- Make the DualSense the primary working surface, not decorative content.
- Put state on the object that owns it: controls illuminate on their real geometry and the controller light reflects the highest-priority activity.
- Preserve flow by keeping mapping edits, layer state, and status in one workspace.
- Be explicit about capability and permission boundaries; never imply that unavailable hardware or access is working.
- Prefer familiar desktop controls and keyboard accessibility over invented interaction patterns.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Every 3D control has a semantic button equivalent, a visible keyboard focus state, and a non-color-only selected/pressed state. The interface supports reduced motion, high-contrast text, zoom/resizing, full keyboard navigation, and assistive descriptions for controller, light, and permission status.
